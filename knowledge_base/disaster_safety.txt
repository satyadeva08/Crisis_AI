FLOOD SAFETY AND RESCUE
Floods can cause drowning, electrical hazards, structural damage, contaminated water, blocked roads, and vehicle entrapment.
Immediate flood safety actions:
- Move affected people to higher and safer ground.
- Do not walk through moving flood water.
- Do not drive through flooded roads.
- Avoid contact with electrical equipment or fallen electrical wires.
- Deploy rescue boats when people are stranded in areas inaccessible by road.
- Prioritize trapped people, children, elderly people, injured people, and people requiring medical assistance.
- Establish evacuation routes away from rapidly rising water.
- Coordinate rescue teams with local emergency authorities.
- Provide medical assistance to injured or exposed victims.
- Avoid entering damaged buildings until they are assessed for structural safety.

EARTHQUAKE SAFETY
Earthquakes can cause building collapse, falling objects, fires, damaged utilities, injuries, and people becoming trapped.
Immediate earthquake safety actions:
- Move away from damaged structures.
- Protect yourself from falling objects.
- Prioritize people trapped under debris.
- Avoid damaged electrical wires and gas lines.
- Establish a safe evacuation area.
- Deploy search and rescue teams.
- Provide medical assistance to injured people.

CYCLONE AND WINDSTORM SAFETY
Cyclones can cause strong winds, flooding, structural damage, fallen trees, power failures, and injuries.
Immediate cyclone safety actions:
- Move people to designated shelters or safe buildings.
- Keep people away from windows and damaged structures.
- Evacuate areas at risk of flooding.
- Avoid fallen electrical wires.
- Deploy rescue teams after dangerous conditions are assessed.
- Prioritize injured and vulnerable people.

LANDSLIDE SAFETY
Landslides can bury roads, buildings, and people and can cause secondary landslides.
Immediate landslide safety actions:
- Keep people away from unstable slopes.
- Evacuate areas at risk of additional movement.
- Deploy search and rescue teams.
- Check for trapped victims.
- Avoid entering unstable structures.
- Establish safe access routes.

NIGHT-TIME RESCUE OPERATIONS (TIME ESCALATION)
Rescue operations conducted at night or in extremely low-visibility conditions are inherently more dangerous and slower.
Immediate night-time actions:
- Deploy portable heavy lighting equipment, thermal imaging cameras, and night-vision drones.
- Do not deploy un-tethered rescue personnel in fast-moving water or highly unstable terrain at night.
- Maintain continuous radio contact with all deployed teams.
- Mark safe zones with highly visible reflective markers or strobe lights.
- Automatically escalate severity due to visibility hazards and delayed response capabilities.

URBAN DISASTER OPERATIONS (CITY/DENSE LOCALITY)
Disasters in densely populated urban areas involve high risks of mass casualties, stampedes, and cascading infrastructure failure.
Immediate urban actions:
- Prioritize high-rise building evacuation and rapid structural assessment.
- Anticipate severe traffic blockages; deploy helicopters, drones, or motorcycles for rapid medical access.
- Secure broken underground gas mains and high-voltage power grids immediately to prevent secondary fires or explosions.
- Set up localized triage centers outside the immediate collapse zone.

RURAL AND REMOTE DISASTER OPERATIONS (ISOLATED LOCALITY)
Disasters in rural, mountainous, or remote localities often suffer from significantly delayed response times and lack of local medical infrastructure.
Immediate rural actions:
- Deploy off-road vehicles (ATVs), air-lift helicopters, and heavy debris-clearing equipment.
- Establish satellite or offline radio communications, as cellular networks are highly vulnerable in isolated areas.
- Advise affected populations to move to pre-identified high ground or community shelters rather than wait for immediate extraction.
- Deploy self-sustaining medical response teams with bulk supplies.

INDUSTRIAL AND CHEMICAL HAZARD OPERATIONS
When disasters strike factories, warehouses, or industrial zones, the risk of toxic exposure is extreme.
Immediate chemical actions:
- Establish an exclusion zone and evacuate everyone downwind of the incident.
- Rescue personnel must wear Level A HAZMAT suits.
- Identify the specific chemicals involved before applying water, as water can react violently with certain industrial materials.
- Treat toxic gas leaks or chemical fires as Critical Severity immediately.

FIRE SAFETY AND RESCUE (URBAN/BUILDING LOCALITY)
Fires in dense residential or commercial buildings spread rapidly and carry severe risks of smoke inhalation, structural collapse, and mass casualties.
Immediate urban fire actions:
- Evacuate the burning building and all immediately adjacent structures.
- Deploy high-capacity aerial ladder firetrucks and multiple EMS units.
- Identify and secure highly flammable materials or gas lines in nearby structures.
- Scan windows for trapped residents.
- Escalate to High or Critical Severity immediately based on building height and density.

WILDFIRE AND FOREST FIRE SAFETY (RURAL/NATURE LOCALITY)
Wildfires and forest fires spread unpredictably due to wind and consume natural vegetation, threatening isolated properties and wildlife.
Immediate wildfire actions:
- Map the fire perimeter, wind direction, and speed to establish large-scale evacuation zones.
- Deploy aerial water bombers and specialized wildland fire crews.
- Create firebreaks by clearing trees and brush ahead of the fire's path.
- Warn populations in the projected path about severe air quality drops and smoke inhalation risks.
- If the fire is nearing a town or residential zone, escalate to Critical Severity immediately.
